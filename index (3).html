Section 2: AI-Supported Feedback

Here we look at Question 7 (a two-step word problem). Four students answered it. The AI wrote feedback for each answer. Then the teacher checked the feedback.

Question 7

A school buys 5 boxes of crayons. Each box has 12 crayons. Then the school gives 18 crayons to another class. How many crayons are left? Show your steps.

Model answer

First, I multiply 5 × 12 = 60 to find how many crayons there are in all. Then I subtract the 18 crayons that were given away: 60 − 18 = 42. So, 42 crayons are left.

Key points

  • Multiply 5 × 12 = 60 (equal groups).
  • Subtract the 18 crayons given away: 60 − 18 = 42.
  • Write the final answer with a unit: 42 crayons.

Score guide (out of 4): 4 = correct and clearly explained · 3 = correct but missing explanation · 2 = some correct ideas, one mistake in thinking · 1 = needs a lot of help.

Student A

“First I multiplied 5 × 12 to find all the crayons: 5 × 12 = 60. Then I subtracted the 18 crayons that were given away: 60 − 18 = 42. So 42 crayons are left.”

AI feedback

Score: 4 / 4

Strength: You wrote “First I multiplied 5 × 12 to find all the crayons,” which clearly shows your plan.

To improve: Your answer is complete and correct. To check it, estimate: 60 − 18 is about 60 − 20 = 40, so 42 makes sense.

Next step: Try a three-step problem and explain each step the same way.

Teacher review: what I changed

I changed the “To improve” tip. The AI suggested the estimate 5 × 10 = 50, but that does not check the final answer (42). I replaced it with 60 − 18 ≈ 60 − 20 = 40, which is a better check for the last step. I kept the score 4/4 because the answer is correct and clearly explained.

Student B

“5 × 12 = 60. 60 − 18 = 42. The answer is 42.”

AI feedback

Score: 3 / 4

Strength: You found the right answer, and “5 × 12 = 60” is correct.

To improve: Your answer does not say what the numbers mean. Add words to explain each step and write “crayons.”

Next step: Rewrite your answer in 2–3 sentences, starting with “First I…”.

Teacher review: what I changed

I did not change this feedback. I checked the math (5 × 12 = 60, 60 − 18 = 42) and the score. 3/4 matches my score guide: the answer is correct, but the steps are not explained and there is no unit.

Student C

“I tried 12 − 18 but you cannot do that, so I did 18 − 12 = 6. Then I multiplied 6 × 5 = 30. So 30 crayons are left.”

AI feedback

Score: 2 / 4

Strength: You showed every step, and “6 × 5 = 30” is a correct multiplication.

To improve: The 18 crayons were given away from all the crayons, not from one box, so find the total first.

Next step: Draw 5 boxes of 12 crayons, count them all, then take away 18.

Teacher review: what I changed

I changed the strength. The AI praised the student for noticing “you cannot” take 18 from 12. But the student then reversed the numbers (18 − 12), so that praise was too generous. I praised the correct multiplication 6 × 5 = 30 instead. The “To improve” part and the score 2/4 stayed the same.

Student D

“5 + 12 = 17. 17 + 18 = 35. The answer is 35 crayons.”

AI feedback

Score: 1 / 4

Strength: Nice start! You added correctly: “5 + 12 = 17” is right.

To improve: “5 boxes” with “12 each” means equal groups, so multiply. “Given away” means take away, so subtract.

Next step: Draw the story: 5 boxes with 12 crayons in each box. Then ask yourself: what happens when 18 crayons leave?

Teacher review: what I changed

I changed the next step. The AI said to underline key words and pick an operation for each one. This can teach students to guess from words only, and it does not work in every problem. I replaced it with drawing the story and asking what is happening. The score 1/4 stayed the same.

Prompts Used

These are the two prompts given to the AI. Click “Copy” to copy a prompt.

Prompt 1: Quiz and answer key

You are an experienced Grade 6 math teacher and assessment designer. Create a quiz called "Math Word Problems" for Grade 6 students (ages 11-12).

Quiz rules:
- 8 questions in total: 6 multiple-choice and 2 short-answer.
- Multiple-choice: 4 options (A-D), only ONE correct answer. Do not use "all of the above" or "none of the above".
- Short-answer: students answer in 2-3 sentences and show their solution steps.
- Topics: addition/subtraction, multiplication/division, fractions, percentages, and two-step problems.
- Difficulty: 3 easy, 3 medium, 2 harder. Include 1 real-life application problem.
- Each question tests ONE clear idea. Use clear wording and no tricks.
- Use short, simple language suitable for Grade 6.

Then create an Answer Key. For each question give:
1. The correct answer.
2. A one-sentence explanation.
3. For multiple-choice: why the most tempting wrong option is wrong.
4. For short-answer: a model solution and 2-3 key points.

Before you answer, double-check every calculation and make sure each multiple-choice question has exactly one correct option.

Prompt 2: Sample answers and feedback

Use this short-answer question for Grade 6 students:
"A school buys 5 boxes of crayons. Each box has 12 crayons. Then the school gives 18 crayons to another class. How many crayons are left? Show your steps."
Model answer: 5 x 12 = 60, then 60 - 18 = 42, so 42 crayons are left.

Task 1: Write 4 realistic student answers (2-3 sentences each):
- 1 strong answer
- 1 good but incomplete answer
- 1 average answer with one misconception
- 1 weak answer
Do not label or point out the mistakes inside the answers.

Task 2: For each student answer, write AI feedback with:
- one specific strength (quote the student's own words),
- one thing to improve (explain the gap or misconception),
- one concrete next step,
- a score out of 4.
Tone: encouraging and age-appropriate for Grade 6. Maximum 60 words per answer.

Check that the scores match the quality of the answers and that all math is correct.
